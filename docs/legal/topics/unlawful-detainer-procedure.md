# Unlawful detainer procedure

> **Not verified legal advice.** Written 2026-09-18 from the firm's site as indexed and general knowledge; every citation below has `verified_on: null` in `../statute-index.md`. Verify before use (see `../README.md`).

**What the firm covers.** The whole eviction lawsuit from notice to lockout, as drawn on the Unlawful Detainer Game Board: notice types, filing, service, motion to quash, demurrer, answer, default and relief, discovery, summary judgment, trial, judgment, writ and sheriff, appeal and stays, removal to federal court for foreclosure tenants, and suing the landlord. Site copy: "Nonpayment of rent is actually the easiest kind of case for the tenant to win"; "the time in eviction cases is spent addressing the mistakes made by the landlord, his lawyer, the court clerks and the judges."

**Statutes relied on (see the index).** CCP §1161 (grounds, notices), §1162 (service of notice), §1166 (complaint), §1167 / §1167.3 / §1167.4 (response times, motion to quash timing), §418.10 (motion to quash, writ), §430.10 (demurrer), §1170 (answer), §1170.5 (trial within 20 days), §1170.7 (summary judgment), §1170.8 (discovery cut-off), §2030-§2033 (discovery), §473(b) / §473.5 / §473(d) (relief from default, vacate, clerical error), §1174 and §715.010 (judgment, writ, 5-day notice), §1176 (stay pending appeal), §1179 (relief from forfeiture), §1013 / §12a (time computation, court days, holidays), §415.46 (prejudgment claim), 28 U.S.C. §1441-1447 (removal / remand).

**2026 currency flags.** ⚠ **AB 2347**: response period is **10 court days** (not 5) since 2025-01-01; check every video, article, kit and template for "5 days". ⚠ Post-denial response periods (§1167.3, §1167.4) may also have changed. ⚠ Local just-cause ordinances add notice requirements not on the board.
